# Phase and orientation audit, reviewed v0.4.0

The new kernels implement conventional sampled winding, explicitly supplied O(2) transport, cross-spectrum/coherence, and lag association. Their execution evidence is `mathematical_results.json`, with typed raw arrays in `mathematical_inputs.npz`; `run_audit.py` regenerates both from fixed development seeds. These are numerical and mathematical checks, not a confirmatory physical study. Existing `phase/v1.py` and previous freezes retain their exact meanings and bytes.

## Scalar winding and identifiability

Write each nonzero sample as `z_i = a_i exp(i phi_i)`. The normalized endpoint link is `q_ij = exp(i(phi_j-phi_i))`. In a closed ordered loop every endpoint factor appears once with each sign, so `product(q_ij)=1`. Its argument cannot identify integer winding. The executable four-sample counterexample has phases `0, pi/2, pi, -pi/2`: principal increments sum to `2*pi`, while the product remains one. This proof is an elementary derivation, independent of any TORUS premise.

`SAMPLED_WINDING_V2` sums principal edge arguments in `[-pi,pi)` and divides by `2*pi`. It only returns the adjacent integer after checking complete phase support, noise-aware distance from the branch cut, and a dimensionless numerical closure residual. Array order defines the positive loop orientation. Reversing it or conjugating the field reverses winding. A single common phase rotation preserves every edge. Positive amplitude unit changes preserve the estimate when the instrument noise bound and amplitude floor transform with the signal.

Each supplied complex-error bound `b_i` uses the same units as amplitude. If `b_i < |z_i|`, the tangent to its uncertainty disk gives an angular bound `asin(b_i/|z_i|)`. An edge is accepted only when its observed principal increment plus both endpoint bounds stays strictly inside the branch, with the registered numerical margin. A noise standard deviation is not a deterministic bound and cannot be passed without a coverage model. Zero amplitude, noise disks containing zero, unobserved samples, and ambiguous edges produce `INDETERMINATE`; there is no phase-hole filling.

An accepted sampled integer is not by itself the continuous winding. Arbitrarily many turns can occur between measured points. On eight evenly spaced samples, fields with continuous winding +1 and +9 produce identical samples. At 64 samples, the +9 synthetic lift becomes resolvable. The optional `sampling_admissible` assertion means a separate model establishes a nonzero path and true phase increments below pi. It cannot be established from endpoint values alone. Zeros inside the path are compatible with winding; zeros on it invalidate the phase path. Random phase loops often have nonzero sampled winding, measured explicitly in the development audit, so specificity, persistence, and error rates require separate calibration.

Under position-dependent changes `z_i -> exp(i alpha_i) z_i`, links acquire `exp(i(alpha_j-alpha_i))`. A +1 loop can become a constant field by this operation. This disproves unrestricted local-gauge invariance. With a separately meaningful transport `U_ij` from j to i and law `U_ij -> exp(i alpha_i) U_ij exp(-i alpha_j)`, `conj(z_i) U_ij z_j / |z_i z_j|` is invariant. The covariant helper verifies this algebra but cannot certify the provenance of the supplied connection. A connection manufactured from scalar endpoints remains tautological.

## Berry construction and characteristic classes

Fukui, Hatsugai and Suzuki define links using overlaps of normalized Bloch eigenstates over a specified Brillouin zone, then principal plaquette logarithms and an admissibility condition. Their equations 1-14 include the eigenstate and band-gap assumptions. This construction does not license calling scalar optical phase differences Berry curvature. No Berry measurement is implemented or claimed here. [Version 2, 2005](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0503172v2).

Hatcher's Proposition 3.11 relates `w1` to orientability of a specified real vector bundle, while Proposition 3.10 identifies `c1` for complex line bundles over suitable bases. A line bundle's triviality does not fix a chosen connection: on the trivial complex line bundle over a circle, take `A=i*a*dtheta`; curvature is zero but loop holonomy is `exp(-i*2*pi*a)` under the transport convention and can be nontrivial. Therefore zero `c1` does not imply zero holonomy, anomaly cancellation, or a physical claim. This example is our derivation, not an attributed result of TORUS. [Hatcher, version 2.2, printed pages 86-87](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/VBKT/VB.pdf).

## Orientation and the proposed fourteen-step rule

Represent O(2) by `g=(theta,s)` acting on a complex coordinate as `exp(i theta) z` for `s=+1` and `exp(i theta) conj(z)` for `s=-1`. Direct substitution gives `(theta,s)(phi,t)=(theta+s*phi,s*t)`; the code tests equality to successive actions on independent complex inputs. A reflection-type element squares to identity because `theta-theta=0` and `(-1)^2=1`. For any fixed element, `det(g^14)=det(g)^14=+1`; the fourteenth power cannot be orientation reversing. The 28th power also has positive determinant. Neither even-step result depends on the rotation angle.

For varying transitions, parity is the product of their signs. An odd number of reflections reverses orientation. Appending one reflection as step 14 imposes the step at which the reversal occurs; this is tested and labeled a supplied model. Separately, `14*(pi/7)=2*pi` and `28*(pi/7)=4*pi`. Multiplication by -1 is a pi rotation of a scalar and has determinant +1 on its two real components. A director identifies signs and has pi angular periodicity; an ordinary vector does not. A spinorial 4*pi return requires a specified covering representation. Physical reflection, changing coordinate convention, reversing time, and changing an internal sign are distinct transformations. The orientable double cover of a Klein bottle introduces no fourteen-step scale.

`EXPLICIT_O2_TRANSPORT_V2` accepts measured or explicitly modeled transition maps only. Unannotated scalar observations return `MONODROMY_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE_FROM_THIS_OBSERVABLE`. A concrete degeneracy is the real scalar +1: both identity and conjugation leave it unchanged, despite opposite determinants. Measuring two independent frame directions or separately calibrated chart transitions could remove this ambiguity under a specified model. A transition product on one registered loop does not establish the topology of physical space or the complete `w1` class on all cycles.

## Actual temporal observables and uncertainty

`TEMPORAL_CROSS_SPECTRUM_V2` requires strictly increasing, equally spaced measured time with no missing support. It uses nonoverlapping complete segments, constant detrending, a declared periodic Hann or boxcar window, and one-sided density scaling. `Sxy=mean(conj(X)*Y)`, so a positive cross phase means y leads x under the positive cosine phase convention. Coherence is `|Sxy|^2/(Sxx*Syy)`. The module normalizes amplitudes before products and records scales to restore dimensional densities. A zero spectral denominator is undefined. The Fourier frequency units are inverse supplied time units; period tests vary physical period and sampling interval. [Official CSD convention](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.csd.html), [official coherence definition](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.coherence.html).

A registered Fourier bin is distinguished from an exploratory selected peak. Optional phase uncertainty resamples whole independent segments, returning circular offsets around the observed phase and preserving the wrap boundary. It requires an explicit exchangeability/independence assertion and a registered bin. Segment count and nonoverlap alone do not justify independence; low coherence or diffuse phase makes this percentile approximation unreliable. Its empirical coverage must be checked for the intended generator or acquisition model.

The antisymmetric lag statistic is `[mean(x_t*y_(t+l))-mean(y_t*x_(t+l))]/(2*sd(x)*sd(y))` after global centering. It reverses sign under time reversal and changes under time permutation; a frame median does not. It measures time-ordered linear association, not causality or T_e. The circular paired moving-block bootstrap reports conditional acquisition-local percentile intervals and sensitivity for block lengths 16, 32, and 64. Artificial joins alter short lags, so block length must substantially exceed the lag. Neither interval construction has been promoted to a universal coverage theorem.

The segment phase surrogate randomizes y's interior Fourier phases independently in each segment while preserving its raw segment periodogram, mean and energy. It destroys registered phase relationships; it does not preserve the marginal amplitude distribution, boundary continuity, or a subsequently Hann-windowed periodogram. Global phase randomization of one pure sinusoid would preserve its coherence and is not used as a false null.

Integrated autocorrelation uses `tau_factor=1+2*sum(rho(k))`, before the first nonpositive estimated lag, and reports `n/tau_factor` only as an estimator-specific approximation under supplied stationary, short-memory, nonoscillatory assumptions. This convention is twice one that begins with one-half. The fixed max-lag is declared. Strong negative lobes or no truncation cause abstention. The estimator cannot establish stationarity or independent systems; drift, long memory and oscillatory fields require separately registered analysis. A stationary AR(1) example checks the analytic `(1+phi)/(1-phi)` factor. An oscillatory example explicitly abstains.

## Reproduction and boundaries

Run `python studies/v0.4.0/reviewed/math/run_audit.py`, then `python -m pytest tests/test_phase_v2.py`. The 26 executed tests also cover direct DFT comparison, Fourier null preservation, missing/gapped support, conditional uncertainty, and scale transformations. `primary_references.json` records inspected sections, versions, response byte counts and independently fetched SHA-256 values. No third-party paper bytes are redistributed.

These results establish algebraic identities and limited instrument behavior. They establish no preferred 14/28 period, physical Klein bottle, anomaly cancellation, anti-time, TORUS-specific incremental value, independent external replication, or confirmatory field result. Those claims remain separate from the implemented conventional kernels and their calibration.
