# Representation Invariance and Equivariance

Representation checks test whether a conclusion depends on an arbitrary faithful encoding.

An invariant endpoint should remain within a registered tolerance after an equivalent transformation. An equivariant endpoint may transform predictably—for example, a direction vector may rotate with coordinates while its magnitude and calibrated separation remain stable. The expected relationship is registered before computation.

Required controls include coordinate rotations or reflections where physically valid, alternate faithful projections, axis-order or indexing checks, and at least one deliberately lossy representation expected to fail or become inconclusive. A passing lossy control is a warning, not extra confirmation.

Agreement is evaluated at the level justified by the estimand: sign, ranking, closure mode, calibrated effect interval, or a transformation law. Scores from Fourier, wavelet, graph, spatial, temporal, or multimodal channels are not averaged unless a common scale has been prospectively calibrated.

A disagreement ledger preserves every failed pair and distinguishes expected equivariance, information-loss disagreement, null incompatibility, implementation error, and unresolved scientific disagreement. Binary Methods A and B fail representation agreement when fewer than two faithful projections agree. Method C retains the separate results and reports the disagreement.
